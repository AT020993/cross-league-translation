## In plain words

* **The paper's sample drops benched signings.** It keeps only arrivals with at least 8 continental games. Lowering that floor to one appearance adds 134 arrivals with 1–7 games to the 674 (16.6% of 808): 110 with 3–7 games and 24 with 1–2. They are 101 EuroCup and 33 EuroLeague signings. They came in with a lower source EFF/36 (mean 16.35 against 17.44) and lower source minutes per game (20.4 against 22.7).
* **Unweighted, the headline pattern holds on the wider sample.** On the 720 arrivals with ≥ 3 games and ≥ 60 minutes, history vs history + translation is 0.122 EFF/36 [0.043, 0.201]; E1 C_H is 0.013 [-0.023, 0.050]; once the leagues are known translation adds -0.001 [-0.025, 0.023].
* **Minutes-weighted, the pattern does not hold (failing: history_gain).** Counting each arrival by his destination minutes, history vs history + translation is 0.087 EFF/36 [-0.010, 0.185] (spans zero; 80%-power detectable effect at this n 0.139); C_H 0.038 [-0.003, 0.079]; λ10 increment -0.017 [-0.044, 0.011], upper bound 0.39% of that model's error. The licence needs both versions, so "the result is not an artefact of the 8-game rule" is **not earned**; under the amendment the failing contrast is the paper's main qualification and has been flagged to Amir.
* **The same contrast also fails minutes-weighted on the original 674.** There the minutes-weighted history gain is 0.098 [-0.003, 0.198], against 0.161 unweighted. So the qualification comes from weighting by minutes, not from the 8-game rule: the gain is smaller for the players who play most.
* **Playing time.** For the share of a full-time slot T (all 808 arrivals, mean T 0.439), offsets λ10 against history: 0.0152 [0.0109, 0.0194] (MAE 0.1247 → 0.1095, 12.2% of history's error). "knowing the leagues also predicts how much a signing will play" is **earned**. Knowing only the destination competition gives 0.0137 [0.0096, 0.0178], 90% of that gain. The EFF/36 translated feature adds 0.0003 [0.0000, 0.0006] on top of offsets λ10.
* **Rotation rate.** 460 of 808 arrivals (56.9%) became rotation players (≥ 15 minutes per game and ≥ 8 games); by source league the rate runs from 43.9% to 67.9%.

## How we got here: the first run failed G14

The first retained run (`research-runs/sloan-fullpaper/survivorship-01`) used Amendment 1's rule literally: drop stints under the new floor of 1, then keep the largest-minutes stint. G14 failed by one key: 673 of 674 anchor keys matched, 1 missing. Brandon Taylor (destination season 2025) had eurocup: 4 games, 113.5 minutes; euroleague: 11 games, 82.6 minutes. v10 uses his EuroLeague stint (11 games); the literal rule picked EuroCup (4 games) because it had more minutes. The generator stopped there and computed nothing else.

Amendment 2 was then written and pushed alone, before any E14 outcome, contrast or profile was computed. It keeps v10's stint for any player with a ≥ 8-game stint and applies the ≥ 1-appearance rule only to players without one. It kept G14 unchanged and added a control: the literal rule must still fail. It does (table above).

## Limitations

* Exploratory: the 2020–2025 outcomes were seen for other analyses before E14 was planned. E14 values themselves were computed only after Amendment 2 was pushed.
* Minutes weighting puts most weight on the players who play most. E14b does not say which weighting a club should prefer; it prints both, and the licence needs both.
* T divides by the team minutes of the games the player appeared in, so it measures the share of a full-time slot when he played, not how often he was left out of the squad.
* Rows with non-positive destination EFF/36 stay excluded (v10 rule), and the source floor stays at 8 domestic games, so the population is wider but still not every signing.
* Translation enters unpenalised while source offsets are penalised, which tilts every increment toward translation; it strengthens the ~0 increment rather than weakening it.
