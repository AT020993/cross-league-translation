**This amendment registers nothing in advance.** Every result it touches was known when it was written. It is the dated record of changes to wording, to the numbers of tables and to the drawing of four figures. It adds no analysis and no contrast.

## Why this exists, in plain words

On 27 November 2026 Amir asked for one more reading of the final paper: whether it is written in an academic register throughout, whether the title is clear, and whether the figures can be improved. He also reported that he could not find Table A26. The table stood between Tables A16 and A17, because the appendix tables carried the numbers they got when they were added and not the numbers of their places.

Three reviews of render 59 were made that day.

* **Title.** Nine candidate titles were rated and ranked by simulated readers (calls to a language model, sixteen for each title; not people). The current title was the first choice of ten of sixteen readers who had also read the abstract. Amir kept it. **The title does not change.**
* **Wording.** A reader went through the 335 text units of the main text, marked 79, and proposed new wording for 52. Each proposal was compared with the registration that fixes or describes the old words. Thirty-seven were put to Amir, who accepted all. Three were dropped while the changes were being made, because a gate or a registered sentence stood in the way. A fresh reader and a claim auditor then read the paper as composed with the rest (see the exposure disclosure). After their reports five more were dropped and five were worded again (numbers 6, 8, 12, 23 and 25). Numbers 1, 2, 3 and 26 were shortened while the two readers were at work, so that the abstract keeps its 264 words; both judged the shortened forms. The caption of one appendix figure changes with its axis label. Twenty-seven passages remain (rule 1); rule 4 lists what was dropped.
* **Figures.** Six of the ten figures are untouched. Three print values that the reader had to estimate or that differed in the last digit from the table beside them, and Figure A3 takes the new axis label of Figure 1 (rule 5).

**What this amendment does not claim.** A blind comparison by 48 simulated readers of the first two pages in the old and the new wording found no reliable difference in how the text was rated. The passages those readers called stiff are for the most part labels and limits that the registrations require, and these stay. The rewording removes commands to the reader, sentences without a verb and colloquial phrases. No gain in readability was measured.

## What has already been seen (exposure disclosure)

* **The author has seen every result of the paper,** as Amendments 28 to 30 say.
* **The readers were calls to a language model, not people.** The simulated readers of the title and of the blind comparison saw the title, the abstract or the first two pages of render 59 with their real values. The reader of the wording worked from render 59, its template, the registrations and the fixtures of gate G19c and of Amendments 29 and 30. The reviewer of the figures redrew them in a working copy from the committed files. The fresh reader and the claim auditor saw render 59 and the composed draft with their real values; the claim auditor also had the registrations and those fixtures. Neither of these two opened a result file or a web page.
* **A rewritten version of the prose, made outside the pipeline on 27 November 2026, was examined that day and not used.** It kept 79 of 677 sentences word for word and no longer held 83 of the 92 registration quotations that stand in render 59. Its numbers equalled those of render 59. The paper was not built from it. One passage of this amendment partly coincides with its wording (number 24, "consists of players whom clubs chose to sign"); every passage of rule 1 was checked on its own against render 59 and the registrations.
* **Draft renders of the paper with these changes were made in a working copy.** They were not retained as runs of record, committed, merged or published.
* **The changes were drafted, and the paper composed with them, before this file was written.** The list of rule 1 is the sentence-by-sentence difference between render 59 and the composed text.
* **Nothing here is a test.** No outcome is read, no estimate is computed and no generator is run.
