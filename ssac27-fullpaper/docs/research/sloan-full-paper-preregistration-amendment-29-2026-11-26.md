**This amendment registers nothing in advance.** Every result it touches was known when it was written. It is the dated record of changes to wording, made in the open so that a reader can see when they were made and why. It adds no analysis and no contrast. Rule 1 changes registered words of the first page after the results were known; that is a change a reader must be able to see, and this file lists it sentence by sentence with the rule of each earlier amendment it touches.

## Why this exists, in plain words

On 26 November 2026, after the paper was public, Amir asked for a second review of the whole paper. Five readers who had not seen it before read render 57: one checked every number against the paper itself and the committed result files and opened the ten figures, one followed every pointer and every statement about a registration, one re-opened the references at their sources, one read every sentence for language, and one drafted answers to a judge's criticisms of the first page. **No result of the paper is wrong.** The readers found:

* five mistakes in wording, two of which Amendment 28 had brought in that morning: an arrival was defined with eight continental games while the paper counted 808 "arrivals", 134 of them with fewer; and the Limitations named five leagues whose 2025-26 data stop early while a registered label in Table A21 names four. The other three: the reference to Amendment 21 said "two rounds" of blind readings where that amendment says three; the paper said that an analyst's pages "were read in full" on a day on which, by the paper's own ledger, part of one page was read; and a read-out that Amendment 15 registered for the frozen 2026-27 test is in the committed score record and was neither printed nor mentioned;
* about thirty-five places where a reader can misread or must read twice;
* that one analyst's page holds numbers which it does not show (rule 4).

A judge's reading of the paper had also criticised the first page: it did not say what the paper tests or what is new, gave no yardstick for the slope and no rule for a club, and stated one gain without its size. One reader drafted a first page that answers those points without a new number, a changed interval or a removed caution. Three further readers, who did not know which page was which and read them in mixed order, then scored both pages as conference judges. The check was fixed before they read: the page changes only if at least two of three prefer the proposal and no score falls. All three preferred it. Their scores, current page to proposed page: novelty 5 to 6 (all three); application 4 to 6 (all three); clarity 5 to 6, 5 to 7 and 5 to 6; rigour 7 to 8, 7 to 7 and 8 to 8. All three gave both pages the verdict "borderline". These are simulated readers of one page; their scores are opinions and not evidence about any result. The reader who drafted the page wrote that three of the judge's objections cannot be met by wording: the abstract still ends on a test without a verdict, the table is not shown to be better than a table printed in 2015, and no decision is shown to change for the better. This amendment does not claim otherwise.

Two sentences of rule 1 differ from the page the three readers saw, each in answer to a point they themselves marked in that page: the sentence on the slope keeps its definition (all three wrote that the draft put the definition off to Results), and the paragraph for a club says "The forecast for a single player" and that its error band is wider than the gap (one wrote that "One forecast" can be misread and that the gap was given without an interval). These two sentences were not read by them.
