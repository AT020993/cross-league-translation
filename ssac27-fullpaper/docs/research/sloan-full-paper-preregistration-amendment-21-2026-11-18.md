This amendment changes wording and placement. It adds no analysis and no number. **Both rules make the paper claim less.**

## Why this exists, in plain words

In three rounds, nine blind readers read the paper. In the last two rounds every reader marked the same sentence of the conclusion as claiming too much: "one number per league **matches** the measured benefit of translation factors". "Matches" reads as a claim that the two are equal. The paper says elsewhere that it never claims that from the frozen test, and that the margin behind the registered statement was set after the interval had been seen. Amendment 13 took the word out of the abstract and the introduction and kept it in the conclusion. This amendment takes it out of the conclusion as well. The registered statement stays in Results, with its margin and that disclosure, as before.

Readers also noted that the first page gives the slope of the league-changes test with the interval that holds the table fixed (0.69 to 0.84), while the wider interval with the table's own values redrawn (0.43 to 0.93) stands only in the body. This amendment lets the first page show the wider interval too.

## What has already been seen (exposure disclosure)

* **E30 has been scored**, once (retained run `league-changes-score-01`, 18 November 2026): registered case "Clear"; slope 0.77 (95% interval 0.69 to 0.84); 0.43 to 0.93 with the table values redrawn (an approximation, read for its width only; Amendment 17, rule 6).

* **Three rounds of blind mock readings exist** (18 November). They are opinions of simulated readers, not evidence about the result.
* **No result of the frozen 2026-27 test exists**, and no 2026-27 statistic, roster or signing file has been read.

## The rules

### 1. The conclusion states translation's increment without the word "matches" (replaces the last sentence of Amendment 13, rule 3)

The conclusion states translation's increment as the abstract and the introduction do: on top of the league numbers a translation factor adds nothing detectable, with the estimate and the interval for the arrivals of 2020–2025 and for the 180 older arrivals, in the form of Amendment 13, rule 2. The conclusion does not use the word "matches" and does not carry the margin.

The conclusion may still say, as a plain fact with its two counts, that the league numbers need only past arrivals and not the same-season pairs that translation factors need. It does not call this "less data" as a merit.

The registered equivalence statement, its margin, the fact that the margin was set after the interval was seen, and the E28c sentence stay where they are in Results, unchanged (Amendment 13, rule 3; Amendment 19, rule 11). No registered sentence leaves the paper.

### 2. The wider interval on the first page (adds to Amendment 19, rule 1, and to Amendment 20, rule 4)

The introduction may add, in the sentence that gives the lowest leave-one-league-out slope or in the next, the interval of the slope with the ten table values redrawn, named as such and as an approximation. Figure 1's right panel may print the same interval as text. It is not drawn as a band. It stays "read for its width only" and does not decide the case (Amendment 17, rule 6). It does not enter the abstract, whose limit of nine numbers stays.

## Gates

* **G21a.** The word "matches" does not occur in the abstract, the introduction or the conclusion; the registered equivalence statement with its margin occurs in Results. **Negative control:** a text with the old conclusion sentence is refused; a text without the registered statement in Results is refused.
* **G21b.** Wherever the redrawn-table interval is printed on the first page or in Figure 1, the same sentence or label names it as the interval with the table values redrawn. **Negative control:** a text that prints it as "the 95% interval" of the slope is refused.
