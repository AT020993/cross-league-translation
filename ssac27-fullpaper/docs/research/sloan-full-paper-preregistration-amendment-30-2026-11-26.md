**This amendment registers nothing in advance.** Every result it touches was known when it was written. It is the dated record of corrections to wording. It adds no analysis and no contrast.

## Why this exists, in plain words

The plan for Amendment 29 held a last step: after the changes are in the paper, one reader who has not seen the paper before checks only what changed, against the rest of the paper. He did this on 26 November 2026 on the paper as composed with Amendment 29 (the difference from render 57, sentence by sentence, computed by himself). **He found no wrong number, interval or count, and no caution that left the first page.** He found four mistakes in the new wording, all small, fourteen places that can be misread and four points of style, and gave an exact replacement for each.

Amir chose to correct the four mistakes and the places that can be misread, except those that rule 4 names. Because Amendment 29 was already pushed and lists its sentences word for word, the corrections are listed here in the same form. The corrected text was then given to the same reader, who checked each correction against the corrected paper and found each of his points settled and one new ambiguity, from a moved sentence; that is corrected too (number 6 gives the final order).

Four of the corrections are on the first page (numbers 1 to 4). They were read by this one reader and not by the three blind readers whose comparison Amendment 29 reports. Each of them makes a statement narrower or plainer: "sets … against" for "tests", a definition for a loose yardstick, "can do" for "does", and an instruction that names the equation.
